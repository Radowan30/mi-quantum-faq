Capability 01

The AI assistant

A quantum-savvy AI partner built into the editor. It doesn't just chat — it draws, plans, and drives the platform's engines for you.

Q.What can the AI assistant actually do?›

Think of it as a quantum expert sitting next to you. It can explain concepts at any level, plan your work as a step-by-step flowchart, write code, and draw live visuals right inside the conversation. Because it's built into a full IDE, it can also read your files and run the platform's quantum engines on your behalf.

Q.Can it explain quantum computing to someone who's brand new?›

Yes — that's one of its best uses. Ask "explain superposition" or "what is a qubit?" and it gives a clear, accurate answer pitched to you, often with a diagram to make it click. It's designed to close the knowledge gap for newcomers and non-specialists, while staying technically sound for experts.

Q.Can it help me plan a research project?›

Absolutely. Ask it to "give me a plan to research <your topic>" and it produces a visual flowchart of the steps — from framing the question to running experiments to writing up. You can refine it in conversation until it matches how you actually work.

Q.Can it create figures I can use in reports and papers?›

Yes. The assistant renders live, interactive visuals directly in the chat and lets you take them full-screen and download them:

Quantum circuitsBloch spheres (3D)Flow & process diagramsCharts & histogramsMath notation

So a question like "design a 3-gate circuit," "show this state on a Bloch sphere," or "plot these measurement counts" becomes a clean graphic ready for a slide, a report, or a research paper.

Q.What are "Agentic" and "Dev" modes?›

Two ways to work, one click apart. Agentic mode is a clean, full-screen chat experience — familiar to anyone who's used an AI assistant — for an AI-first workflow. Dev mode is a full professional IDE with file explorer, editors and tools, for hands-on development. The Optimizer and Synthetic-data dashboards open as tabs in both modes.

Capability 02

The Quantum Optimizer

Many real business problems are "optimization" problems — pick the best combination out of astronomically many. The optimizer turns those into a QUBO — the form quantum and quantum-inspired solvers work on — checks the compiled model before any solver runs, and compares every answer with classical methods.

Q.What kinds of problems does it solve?›

Everyday decisions that get impossibly complex at scale. Five ready-made templates cover the common ones:

Shortest delivery routeLoad selectionNetwork partitionPortfolio selectionTask-to-slot scheduling

For anything else, a model builder lets you define your own variables, objective and constraints. Even a 15-stop delivery route has over 43 billion possible orders — too many to check by hand.

Q.How does it make sure an answer can be trusted?›

When a problem is translated into maths for a solver, a small, invisible mistake can return a confident but wrong answer. The optimizer checks at three points:

  • Before modelling — with the language model switched on, AI checks your one-sentence goal against your data, and asks "Solve anyway?" if they disagree.
  • Before solving — the model is compiled into a QUBO and checked first: every constraint is enforced with a proven penalty weight, and nothing is run until then.
  • After solving — every answer is compared with a classical baseline and an exact classical reference. The result says which method produced it, shows a certificate such as "Optimal (proven)" when optimality is proven, and checks every constraint against your own numbers.
Q.Do I need to be a quantum expert to use it?›

No. You write one sentence describing your goal — in English or Bahasa Melayu — and add your data, starting from a template or the model builder. The platform does the quantum modelling and writes the model out as an equation you can inspect, so you never have to write the underlying maths yourself.

Q.Can I just ask the AI assistant to run it?›

Yes. The optimizer is connected to the AI chat through nine tools, so you can upload data, formulate and check a model, submit a solve and read the result by conversation — or open its dashboard as a tab in the IDE and work hands-on. Same engine, your choice of interface.

Q.Does it over-promise on "quantum"?›

Deliberately not. It runs classical and quantum-inspired solvers, and QAOA — a quantum algorithm — on a classical simulator: no quantum hardware is used and no speedup is claimed. Every job is compared with classical methods, and if one beats the solver's own answer you get the better answer, with the result saying which method produced it. It's built to earn trust in a hype-heavy field, not to inflate it.

Q.Which solvers can it use?›

Seven, and an automatic mode picks one for you:

  • Classical — exact enumeration (small problems), simulated annealing and tabu search on the CPU, and parallel tempering on the GPU.
  • Quantum-inspired — simulated bifurcation and simulated quantum annealing on the GPU.
  • Quantum algorithm — QAOA, run on a simulator.
Capability 03

The Synthetic Data Engine

Good data is often sensitive, scarce, or lopsided. This engine creates realistic artificial data that follows the patterns of the real thing — using quantum generative models, with its quality and privacy risk measured on every run.

Q.What is synthetic data, and why would I want it?›

It's data that looks and behaves like your real data but isn't. That lets you test on it and train models with it when the real data is sensitive (patient or customer records), scarce (not enough examples), or imbalanced (fraud and rare faults are rare by definition). Every run also measures privacy risk — how close the synthetic rows sit to real ones, and a membership-inference test — so you can judge it before you share it.

Q.What can it do?›
  • Generate a realistic look-alike of a table you upload (CSV, Excel, Parquet or JSON), after profiling its quality.
  • Rebalance rare cases — a "Balanced" option oversamples rare classes, such as a rare attack or disease.
  • Synthesise related tables together — it detects the keys between them and checks referential integrity.
  • Find a dataset for you on Hugging Face, data.gov.my or UCI if you have none yet, with its licence recorded.

And it picks the best method automatically by running a tournament of up to 13 engines — 4 quantum families against 9 classical baselines — and keeping the winner on multi-seed scores, with paired t-tests against the runner-up. Each run is scored for fidelity, detector and SDMetrics quality (plus utility when you pick a target column), and a difference that is only noise is labelled "within noise", so you can trust a genuine improvement before it goes into a report or paper.

Q.What is actually "quantum" about it?›

The generative core has four quantum model families — QCBM, QGAN, QVAE and QRBM — built on quantum circuits simulated on a GPU (no quantum hardware). They only "win" if they beat strong classical methods on your data, and the dashboard shows it plainly when a classical engine wins. You can also let the platform evolve the quantum circuit design for your dataset, and see the circuit behind a quantum run drawn on screen.

Q.Can I drive it by chatting with the AI too?›

Yes — like the optimizer, it's connected to the AI assistant through 13 tools, so you can find, profile and generate data in plain English — or open its dashboard as a tab in the IDE and click through it yourself. Its own optional AI helper runs on a local model on the same GPU server.
